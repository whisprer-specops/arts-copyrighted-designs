What this is: a wearable LED cape/poncho + belt/pocket controller + firmware that paints a time-varying dazzle/fractal pattern. Claim is not “invisible to one still.” Claim is “appearance is unstable enough that multi-camera re-ID / track-handoff (Flock-style network stitching) falls apart.” Current BOM-ish ticket: ~$1,000. Question: what price × volume actually maximises profit, and is $1k even in the right universe.
Short answer: $1k is a boutique/halo price, not a volume price. The market that already exists for “clothes vs cameras” is buying $35–$80 printed tees/hoodies, with a thin luxury layer at €350–€670. A glowing $1k poncho is a different product: statement object + firmware platform, not a privacy commodity. You can sell some. You will not sell “enough to be a viable mass product” at $1k without a different buyer than “person who wants to walk past Flock poles unnoticed.”

1. The target system is mostly the wrong animal
Flock’s core product is vehicle capture, not pedestrian invisibility. Falcon-class units motion-trigger stills, OCR the plate, and fingerprint the car (colour, make/body, racks, stickers, damage). Network value is density of those point-reads stitched in software. Official line: not facial recognition, not continuous live tracking. That is half-true.⁠Flockcamera
What matters for your pitch:

Firmware on seized units does run person detection (box + confidence), even if marketing talks plates.⁠WIRED
Condor PTZ exists specifically to follow people.⁠ZDNet
Search tools now include natural-language “find people matching this description in this polygon.”⁠WIRED
Deployed scale: on the order of 100k–120k cameras, thousands of agencies, national lookup if customers opt in. Valuation in the multi-billion range. Backlash is real (dozens of cities cancelling), which is demand-signal for anti-Flock sentiment. It is not demand-signal for a $1k LED cloak.⁠Business Insider

Implication: if the buyer still drives, the cape does almost nothing to the thing Flock is good at. The trackable object is the vehicle fingerprint. If the buyer walks, Flock Falcon is not the main stack — UK live facial recognition vans, private CCTV, bodycams, and Condor-class PTZ are. Your firmware story has to be cross-camera appearance Re-ID, not “Flock can’t see me.”
Re-ID research is explicit: short-horizon tracking leans hard on clothing colour/texture. Change clothes between cameras and match quality collapses; that’s why “clothes-changing Re-ID” is its own research field. A continuously mutating surface is, in principle, a stronger attack on appearance embeddings than a static print — printed adversarial is a fixed signature cameras can eventually train on.⁠Intellisee
That is the actual unique claim. Keep it. Do not oversell “messes with Flock completely.”

3. Who would buy your thing, and what they’ll pay
Four segments. Do not mix their prices.
A. Statement / fashion-tech / collector
Wants the object. Glowing fractal cape is the point. Comp: Cap_able, Enlighted, CuteCircuit-adjacent.
WTP: $400–$1,500. Volume: tens to low hundreds/year if you have press + a beautiful object. Profit lives here if COGS is under ~$350.
B. Privacy maximalists / journalists / activists who already own Faraday gear
Will pay for updateable firmware if you can show Re-ID break on their cameras. They will not pay $1k for a demo on YOLO. They will compare you to a $75 hoodie that claims the same sentence.
WTP: $150–$400 if proven; $50–$120 if “trust us.”
UK extra: Greater Manchester already in the LFR club; Crime and Policing Act 2026 lets police designate mask-free protest zones (up to £1k fine / 1 month). A lit cape at a demo is not stealth — it is a flag. Sell “firmware privacy tool,” not “protest kit.”⁠Www
C. Tactical / “I don’t want to be stitched across an estate camera mesh”
This buyer exists. They buy IR-blocking, Faraday, plate covers (legal mess), ghost-plate folklore. They want low signature to humans. A bright LED poncho is the opposite of that job.
WTP for this form factor: near zero. They’d pay $1k for something that looks like a dull raincoat.
D. Grey market / “evade police”
Exists. Do not build the brand for them. Legal and platform risk eats the company. Also: if it works well enough to market as “defeats Flock/police tracking,” you invite statute, TOS bans, and payment-processor allergy. Kate Bertash / noRecognition already dance around plate-obstruction law by attacking classification, not covering plates.⁠Cryptonomist
Honest TAM for a $1k LED cape in year 1, good press, working demo video: 50–300 units. That’s $50k–$300k revenue. Not a factory. A workshop + Kickstarter + a waiting list.

5. Cost-down reality check
LED cloth BOM is not “LEDs got cheap so capes are $80.”

Fashion LED garments that display patterns sit $200–$600 consumer, $4.5k+ dense handmade.
Addressable density for a flowing fractal that survives 10–20 m camera distance is not a festival hoodie.
Wash, flex, rain, Manchester winter, battery hours, visibility to humans at night, EMI, CE/UKCA, garment flammability — these eat the “mass production will fix it” sentence.
Firmware is the moat. Fabric is a commodity race you will lose to Shenzhen if you compete on glow.

If $1k is today’s true cost, treat it as v1 halo. Plan a $299–$449 v2 that is a vest/poncho panel (smaller emissive area, same controller) rather than full-cloth.

6. Legal / positioning (the one risk that matters)

Printed adversarial clothing is sold openly. Electronic “defeat police cameras” is a hotter sentence.
UK protest law is tightening on identity concealment in designated zones. A cape that advertises evasion is a gift to a cop writing a statement. Sell privacy / anti-Re-ID / anti-profiling, publish methods against commercial models, do not market “beats Greater Manchester Police.”
Platforms and processors will kick you if the landing page says “invisible to Flock.”
Effectiveness decays: Cap_able’s own orbit already admits the arms race; printed garments go stale when models retrain. Dynamic + updateable is the only durable product story. Own that.


7. What I’d actually pitch (unasked, because the $1k full-cape-as-mass-SKU is a hole)
Three SKUs, one firmware:

Printed / dyed “dazzle” poncho or oversized shirt — $79–$129. Volume engine. Same visual language. Ships in weeks. Funds the brand. Comp: noRecognition + Urban Privacy.
Active panel vest or yoke (LED or IR+visible) — $299–$449. The real product. Pocket controller. Updateable patterns. This is where Re-ID-break lives without a $1k cape.
Full LED cloak made-to-order — $999–$1,800. Press object, collectors, installations. Never the volume SKU.

If you only ship #3 at $1k: workshop business, Kickstarter-sized (~$100–$250k if the video slaps), not a clothing line.
If the firmware really does argmax-style moving camo that breaks appearance Re-ID on held-out models and a couple of commercial NVRs, publish the test protocol. That is the only thing that lets you charge 5× a hoodie. Without published tests you are competing with $45 tees on vibes, and vibes lose at $1k.
